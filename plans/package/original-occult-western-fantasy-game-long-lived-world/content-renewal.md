# Long-Lived World — Content Renewal

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. Goal

The long-lived world must remain generative for thousands of turns and many decades without relying on a finite list of preallocated case instances or unrestricted model improvisation.

The core model is:

**curated grammar + current world state + bounded model deliberation + Runtime Authority canonization**

## 2. Curated pattern grammars

Human-authored grammars define playable structures rather than one-off instances.

Candidate families include:

- disappearance;
- homicide;
- fraud;
- property conflict;
- industrial accident;
- inheritance dispute;
- religious conflict;
- institutional struggle;
- identity/document fraud;
- anomaly contamination;
- Claim instability;
- family secret;
- historical cold case;
- corporate abuse;
- migration/displacement conflict;
- factional schism.

A grammar defines structural roles and legal combinations, not fixed names, dates or outcomes.

## 3. World-state composition

Each generated matter should derive as much as possible from current canonical state.

Composition inputs may include:

- living actors;
- archived/dead actors;
- family relations;
- institutions and office holders;
- property and businesses;
- historical hooks;
- unresolved cases;
- Claims;
- anomaly families;
- class and district conditions;
- prior player choices;
- institutional agendas;
- current social tensions.

## 4. Matter structure

A renewable matter may combine:

- causal trigger;
- involved entities;
- competing interests;
- hidden facts;
- occult/anomalous factor;
- institutional involvement;
- time pressure;
- historical ties;
- escalation paths;
- possible resolution families.

The exact schema remains open for implementation design.

## 5. Bounded model deliberation

The model may propose constrained details such as:

- motives;
- testimony phrasing;
- character presentation;
- cover stories;
- plausible minor background;
- scene-level texture.

Runtime Authority must validate and canonize all structural facts.

The model cannot invent contradictory historical facts or bypass eligibility rules.

## 6. Historical Hook Pool

Resolved matters may leave compact reusable hooks.

Examples:

- unpaid debts;
- missing evidence;
- disputed death;
- vanished person;
- hidden heir;
- inherited grudge;
- institutional scandal;
- wrong conviction;
- unexplained anomaly residue;
- unresolved Claim consequence;
- secret archive;
- lost property;
- family story whose truth remains uncertain.

Hooks may remain dormant for years or decades before re-entering active play.

## 7. World-entity lifecycle

Long-lived world entities are not immortal by default.

### Locations

May:

- be built;
- expand;
- change ownership;
- change function;
- rename;
- decline;
- burn;
- be demolished;
- be rebuilt;
- become historically protected;
- disappear from active geography while remaining in archives.

### Businesses and organizations

May:

- be founded;
- grow;
- merge;
- split;
- relocate;
- change purpose;
- collapse;
- dissolve;
- be absorbed;
- leave successor organizations.

### Districts and city structure

May:

- expand;
- densify;
- decline;
- gentrify;
- industrialize;
- deindustrialize;
- rebuild after disaster;
- change social composition;
- gain or lose strategic importance.

The v1 opening map is therefore the initial state of history, not a permanent map.

## 8. Causal creation

New entities require origin causes.

Examples:

- a founder or founding coalition;
- capital or patronage;
- population growth;
- migration;
- infrastructure;
- war/disaster;
- religious schism;
- corporate expansion;
- player-created vacancy;
- political/institutional reform;
- neighborhood demand;
- inherited property.

Generation must be traceable back to world state rather than context-free spawning.

## 9. Anti-repetition

Generation must apply both cooldown and semantic-distance checks.

Similarity dimensions should include at least:

- victim/subject type;
- actor-role configuration;
- institution combination;
- hidden truth;
- anomaly family;
- investigation path;
- stakes;
- resolution structure;
- historical-hook reuse.

Repeated surface nouns with identical underlying structure are not considered novel.

## 10. Ephemeral versus canonical content

### Ephemeral Matter

Routine matters may retain only:

- outcome;
- involved durable actors;
- important consequences;
- hooks;
- compact summary.

### Canonical Event

Promote when a matter materially changes:

- Tier A/B actors;
- institutions;
- geography;
- Claims;
- law or public order;
- family history;
- major property/wealth;
- protagonist identity/exposure;
- long-term social structure.

Canonical events persist as durable history.
