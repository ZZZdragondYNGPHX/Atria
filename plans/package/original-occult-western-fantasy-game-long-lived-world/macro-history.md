# Long-Lived World — Macro History

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. Purpose

The macro layer exists so decades and centuries contain coherent history.

It must create meaningful background change without turning the game into a full grand-strategy simulator.

## 2. Core macro domains

The system should support at least:

- economic cycles;
- governance and law;
- war and geopolitical conflict;
- migration and demographic change;
- public health and disaster;
- religion and social movements;
- large-scale occult events;
- technology/infrastructure diffusion.

## 3. Economic cycles

Economic history should be represented as systemic conditions rather than occasional flavor text.

Possible states include:

- expansion;
- prosperity;
- credit growth;
- inflation;
- bubble;
- recession;
- banking crisis;
- depression;
- recovery.

Local consequences may affect:

- wages;
- unemployment;
- rents;
- land values;
- business revenue;
- credit access;
- bankruptcy;
- migration;
- crime;
- charity/religious relief;
- political pressure.

The engine does not need to simulate every transaction.

## 4. War

War is normally resolved as a macro event graph or campaign state.

Potential phases include:

- tension;
- mobilization;
- war;
- escalation/stalemate;
- armistice;
- reconstruction.

Local effects may include:

- conscription;
- casualty/missing-person outcomes;
- refugees;
- industrial conversion;
- shortages;
- inflation;
- transport disruption;
- accelerated technology;
- emergency authority;
- occult exploitation;
- battlefield-created artifacts/anomalies.

If the protagonist enters a relevant theater, that region may promote into detailed simulation.

## 5. Governance and law

Governance should be represented through:

- offices;
- institutions;
- factions;
- economic interests;
- religious interests;
- labor/social organizations;
- media;
- public pressure.

Laws have history.

A legal rule may move through:

**proposal → dispute → adoption → implementation → amendment/repeal**

The same action may therefore have different consequences in different eras.

## 6. Demography and migration

Macro population is structural, not per-capita.

Track only meaningful aggregates such as:

- population size;
- broad age structure;
- major communities;
- class/employment composition;
- migration inflow/outflow;
- urban density;
- regional settlement patterns.

Individual NPCs are instantiated only when relevance requires it.

## 7. Public health and disaster

Possible macro shocks include:

- epidemic;
- fire;
- flood;
- earthquake;
- famine;
- industrial disaster;
- occult catastrophe.

The important output is structural consequence.

A disaster may lead to:

- population loss;
- migration;
- institutional reform;
- new hospitals;
- legal changes;
- new infrastructure;
- occult policy;
- long-lived scars in geography and memory.

## 8. Religious and social movements

Movements may:

- emerge;
- recruit;
- split;
- radicalize;
- institutionalize;
- decline;
- disappear.

They may alter law, institutions, public pressure, identity risk and occult policy.

## 9. Macro grammar + world state

Macro history should use curated grammars.

Example grammar families:

- recession/crisis;
- war;
- epidemic;
- social movement;
- constitutional/governance crisis;
- religious schism;
- migration wave;
- technological diffusion;
- occult catastrophe.

World state determines:

- participants;
- severity;
- duration;
- affected regions;
- outcomes;
- downstream consequences.

## 10. Relevance gating

Macro events do not automatically become detailed gameplay.

Detailed promotion occurs when an event intersects with:

- protagonist location;
- close relations;
- family;
- property/businesses;
- institutions;
- active Claims;
- chosen interests;
- declared alerts.

Otherwise it may remain summarized macro history.

## 11. Player leverage

Player influence should be measured by actual causal leverage.

Possible scales:

- personal;
- local;
- city;
- regional;
- national;
- international.

Relevant leverage may come from:

- money/capital;
- land/property;
- institutions;
- offices;
- media;
- professional authority;
- organizations;
- networks;
- archives/information;
- occult capability.

Longevity by itself grants no automatic political power.

## 12. Extreme-late-game macro influence

A sufficiently established protagonist may influence:

- city law;
- regional governance;
- national regulation;
- war policy;
- large corporations;
- major religious structures;
- international institutions;
- large occult orders.

This is optional.

A low-profile protagonist may never seek such influence.
