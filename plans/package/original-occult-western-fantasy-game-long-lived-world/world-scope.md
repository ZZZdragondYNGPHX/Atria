# Long-Lived World — World Scope

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. Core principle

The long-lived world is not permanently confined to the opening city.

The starting city is the first high-fidelity simulation hub and historical anchor.

Over decades, the protagonist may relocate, travel, invest, form families, build institutions and maintain influence across multiple regions.

## 2. Regional fidelity tiers

### Active Hub

A region where the protagonist is currently living or operating at high relevance.

Supports:

- detailed geography;
- Tier A/B actors;
- family and household state;
- active institutions;
- property/businesses;
- local law and social conditions;
- cases and renewable matters;
- urban change;
- high-frequency agenda resolution.

### Warm Region

A previously important or still materially connected region where the protagonist is not currently based.

Retains:

- important family/relationships;
- assets;
- institutions;
- major actors;
- unresolved hooks;
- local historical trajectory;
- periodic background resolution.

### Cold World

Low-relevance regions are represented coarsely.

Maintain only what is needed for coherent macro history, such as:

- major cities;
- state/national conditions;
- large institutions;
- wars and crises;
- migration;
- technological level;
- major religious movements;
- important occult developments.

## 3. Promotion and demotion

Regions may move between fidelity tiers.

Promotion occurs when:

- the protagonist relocates;
- sustained investment/relationships demand detail;
- a major case or institution becomes central;
- family or long-term assets require detailed simulation.

Demotion occurs when detailed state is no longer required.

Demotion compacts detail but preserves canonical history and durable consequences.

## 4. Historical materialization

A region promoted to Active Hub must materialize from prior world truth.

If macro history already recorded:

- disaster;
- migration;
- war;
- infrastructure;
- corporate growth;
- religious change;
- institutional reform;

then detailed geography and actors must reflect those facts.

The world does not begin existing when first rendered.

## 5. Travel

Travel consumes real world time.

Duration depends on:

- origin/destination;
- era;
- infrastructure;
- available transport;
- wealth/access;
- disruption such as war, weather or disaster.

Travel may produce absence consequences in other regions.

## 6. Persistent remote interests

The protagonist may hold remote:

- property;
- businesses;
- family connections;
- institutional offices;
- agents;
- archives;
- investments;
- occult sites;
- political/social relationships.

Remote holdings are handled through delegation and lower-frequency resolution where appropriate.

## 7. Macro World Layer

A coarse global layer tracks developments too large for local simulation.

Candidate domains include:

- war;
- economic crises;
- migration;
- technology diffusion;
- national law;
- epidemic/public-health events;
- religious movements;
- large corporations;
- international institutions;
- major occult disasters/discoveries.

Macro developments feed local consequences into relevant hubs.

## 8. Multi-hub long-term play

The protagonist may eventually sustain several important hubs.

Example patterns:

- original city remains family/archive base;
- second city becomes business center;
- third region contains major occult institution;
- overseas region hosts long-running research or allies.

Multiple hubs must not require equal full-fidelity simulation at all times.

## 9. International influence

International scale is possible through accumulated:

- capital;
- agents;
- institutions;
- business networks;
- religious networks;
- archives;
- diplomatic/social contacts;
- occult infrastructure.

Influence is causal and earned.

Longevity alone does not imply world leadership.
