# Long-Lived World — Progression and Continuity

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. Progression philosophy

Century-scale play must not rely on endless numeric escalation.

Progression is split conceptually into:

- **early vertical growth** — ordinary improvement toward mature competence;
- **long-term horizontal growth** — new permissions, relationships, assets, authority, specialized knowledge, occult capabilities and obligations.

A 120-year-old protagonist should feel historically and structurally powerful without requiring absurd skill values.

## 2. Horizontal progression domains

Long-term progression may accumulate through:

- institutional access;
- professional credentials;
- specialized occult techniques;
- Claim transformations;
- property and businesses;
- archives and information networks;
- durable relationships;
- favors and debts;
- legal/social authority;
- ritual infrastructure;
- rare artifacts;
- geographic reach;
- reputation across generations;
- knowledge of recurring anomaly families;
- secret organizations and protected channels.

## 3. Wealth and assets

Wealth is not a permanently increasing score detached from history.

Assets should be represented as world-bound property with provenance.

Potential asset classes include:

- currency/liquid reserves;
- land;
- housing;
- commercial property;
- businesses;
- shares/ownership interests;
- trusts;
- collections/artifacts;
- institutional holdings.

Assets may:

- appreciate;
- depreciate;
- be taxed;
- be seized;
- burn;
- fail;
- be inherited;
- become obsolete;
- be legally stranded by identity change;
- require proxies/trusts/forged succession;
- become evidence of abnormal longevity.

## 4. Career continuity

The protagonist may change careers repeatedly across decades.

Examples may include:

- investigator;
- business owner;
- academic;
- religious official;
- civil servant;
- public office holder;
- institutional administrator;
- researcher;
- retired/low-profile private figure.

Career is primarily a bundle of:

- permissions;
- access;
- social credibility;
- institutional relationships;
- responsibilities;
- legal constraints;
- exposure risk.

It is not a fixed lifetime class.

## 5. Public identity continuity

The persistent protagonist and the current public/legal identity are separate.

A public identity may be:

- retained;
- retired;
- declared dead;
- replaced;
- hidden;
- reconstructed through forged or legitimate succession.

Identity transitions can affect:

- property title;
- banking;
- licenses;
- contracts;
- employment;
- marriage/legal family;
- court records;
- criminal history;
- institutional membership;
- reputation.

Long-term identity management is gameplay, not a cosmetic alias switch.

## 6. Occult power

The protagonist may become significantly more capable over decades.

Power growth should add:

- new domains of action;
- specialized authority;
- resilience;
- rare access;
- new ritual options;
- new forms of survival.

But stronger occult capability should also create some combination of:

- Claim escalation;
- detectability;
- institutional attention;
- countermeasures;
- obligations;
- route-specific vulnerabilities;
- effects on identity/family.

The game should not neutralize growth by simply increasing all enemy numbers.

## 7. Challenge migration

As personal threats become easier, challenge may shift toward:

- family safety and loyalty;
- institutional conflict;
- legal exposure;
- property networks;
- public legitimacy;
- historical evidence;
- political/social consequences;
- city-scale crises;
- Claim obligations;
- ancient or systemic occult threats.

The world acknowledges that the protagonist has become stronger.

## 8. City-scale influence

The protagonist may, through play, become:

- a major business owner;
- substantial landholder;
- institutional leader;
- religious authority;
- social patron;
- information broker;
- occult power broker;
- hidden city-scale influencer.

This outcome is permitted but not required.

A player may instead remain deliberately obscure for a century.

## 9. Embodied vs persistent progress

### Embodied progress

May depend on the current body or public identity:

- physical conditioning;
- body-specific injuries;
- appearance;
- some equipment;
- credentials tied to identity;
- current office;
- current employment;
- current legal access.

### Persistent progress

Normally survives continuity events:

- deep knowledge;
- long-term relationship history;
- Claim state;
- Chronicle/history;
- durable occult contracts where applicable;
- hidden networks;
- long-held secrets;
- world reputation where identity continuity is known.

Bodily death/reconstruction may therefore be costly without deleting the century-scale character.
