# Long-Lived World — Era Evolution

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. Core principle

A century-scale campaign must allow the surrounding civilization to change materially.

The opening occult-western period is an initial historical era, not a permanent aesthetic lock.

Sufficiently long play may enter modern and later technological/social eras.

## 2. Domains of change

Era evolution may affect:

- transportation;
- communications;
- energy;
- urban infrastructure;
- industry;
- business structure;
- finance;
- medicine;
- education;
- policing;
- public administration;
- law;
- media;
- religion;
- social norms;
- housing;
- city planning;
- weapons and security;
- record keeping;
- identity systems.

## 3. Authored inertia + emergent divergence

The world should have curated historical tendencies so development remains coherent.

However, it must not replay a rigid real-world timeline.

Actual development depends on:

- institutions;
- available capital/resources;
- infrastructure;
- wars/disasters;
- migration;
- research;
- occult discoveries;
- regulation;
- player investment or sabotage;
- prior world events.

The protagonist can influence history only through credible causal leverage.

## 4. Occult modernization

Supernatural knowledge evolves alongside ordinary technology.

Possible long-horizon developments include:

- anomaly taxonomy;
- standardized Claim terminology;
- safer ritual protocols;
- occult medicine;
- specialized insurance;
- legal classification;
- professional licensing;
- containment standards;
- industrialized ritual tools;
- anti-anomaly equipment;
- occult communications;
- corporate exploitation of supernatural processes;
- public or secret regulatory regimes.

No specific endpoint is frozen yet.

## 5. Obsolescence and adaptation

Old world structures do not remain viable merely because the protagonist owns or knows them.

Examples:

- transport businesses may become obsolete;
- property values may rise or collapse;
- old professions may shrink;
- institutions may reform;
- laws may eliminate former privileges;
- technologies may make old skills less valuable;
- old occult methods may become standardized, banned or superseded.

Adaptation is gameplay.

## 6. Era as a systemic rule layer

Era transitions may change:

- available careers;
- business types;
- legal permissions;
- institution powers;
- transport networks;
- communication latency;
- artifact classes;
- public-record density;
- identity-management difficulty;
- case grammars;
- social expectations;
- city expansion rules;
- occult countermeasures.

An Era transition is therefore more than a change in year or visual theme.

## 7. Condition-driven transitions

Era changes are not locked to exact dates.

Potential transition requirements may include:

- infrastructure thresholds;
- institution adoption;
- capital availability;
- research milestones;
- public demand;
- crisis/disaster;
- regulatory change;
- occult breakthrough;
- player-driven investment or obstruction.

This allows a recognizable historical trajectory without eliminating alternate history.

## 8. Player influence

The protagonist may accelerate, delay or redirect developments when they actually possess enough:

- capital;
- institutional authority;
- research capability;
- social influence;
- occult leverage;
- infrastructure control.

A single unsupported declaration cannot rewrite the technology tree.

## 9. Era-sensitive content

Renewable content should change with the world.

Opening-era matters may center on:

- rail;
- telegraph;
- early industrial hazards;
- church property;
- local newspapers;
- fragmented record keeping.

Later eras may introduce:

- automobiles;
- electrical infrastructure;
- telephone systems;
- radio;
- large corporate networks;
- modern hospitals;
- denser legal/financial records;
- aviation;
- newer forms of surveillance and identity pressure.

Further eras may continue evolving according to the setting's alternate technological history.
