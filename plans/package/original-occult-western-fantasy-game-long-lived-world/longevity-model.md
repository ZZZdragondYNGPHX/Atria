# Long-Lived World — Longevity Model

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. Core continuity

The player controls one continuous protagonist for the entire save.

The game does not use heir, descendant, student, successor, or replacement-character handoff as its primary long-term continuation model.

The surrounding society may change generations around the protagonist while the player's identity remains continuous.

## 2. Longevity model

Supernatural longevity is a setting-native long-horizon mechanic.

It must not be represented only as a boolean such as `aging=false`.

Normal viable play paths should eventually expose at least one setting-consistent longevity route. Different routes may use different fiction and prices, including Claim transformation, occult contract, anomaly integration, ritual renewal, institutional support, bodily reconstruction, or equivalent mechanisms.

The exact route catalogue remains open.

## 3. Age dimensions

Long-horizon state should distinguish at least:

- **Chronological age** — total lived world time.
- **Apparent age** — current physical presentation.
- **Identity age** — how long the current public/social identity has existed.

These dimensions may diverge.

A protagonist can therefore be chronologically 110 years old, apparently 34, while their current public identity has existed for only 12 years.

## 4. Long-life consequences

Longevity should create slow-burn world pressure rather than repetitive maintenance chores.

Potential consequence families include:

- identity exposure and documentary inconsistency;
- accumulated Claim pressure;
- occult or institutional attention;
- social alienation caused by outliving ordinary people;
- obligations, enemies, debts, property and secrets resurfacing decades later;
- historical records making the protagonist easier to trace;
- route-specific bodily, metaphysical, psychological or reputational costs.

The system should prefer consequences that generate new play over simple periodic upkeep.

## 5. Death semantics

Ordinary lethal outcomes do not automatically end the save.

Possible consequences include:

- severe or permanent injury;
- bodily death followed by occult return;
- reconstruction or replacement of the body while preserving protagonist continuity;
- disappearance for months or years;
- memory damage or fragmented recall;
- loss of resources, property or public identity;
- stronger Claim burden;
- institutional or supernatural exposure;
- world advancement during absence.

Return must not erase the consequences of death.

The world continues independently while the protagonist is absent.

## 6. True Death

A true irreversible death may exist only as an exceptional explicit end-state.

It must not be triggered casually by normal combat, disease, accidents or ordinary ritual failure.

Its exact triggers, opt-in semantics and relationship to deliberate campaign endings remain open.

## 7. Still open

- exact longevity route families;
- whether apparent age freezes, slows, cycles, or varies by route;
- whether resurrection/reconstruction is guaranteed after ordinary death or can itself create branching failure states;
- exact costs and recovery windows;
- interaction between longevity and public/legal identity;
- long-term memory integrity and whether century-scale experience creates special mechanics.
