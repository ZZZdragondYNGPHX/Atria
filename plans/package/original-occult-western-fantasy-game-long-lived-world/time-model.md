# Long-Lived World — Time Model

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. No artificial campaign horizon

The long-lived world has no fixed Day 30, Year 30 or similar authority cutoff.

Practical limits should come from state integrity, lifecycle consequences and available world evolution, not an arbitrary calendar ceiling.

## 2. Time hierarchy

Authoritative long-horizon time should support:

`Scene → Day → Week → Month → Season → Year → Era`

- **Scene** handles immediate action.
- **Day** handles ordinary short-horizon world change.
- **Week** supports short background progression.
- **Month** supports medium-term routines, cases and relationships.
- **Season** supports broader institutional/social change.
- **Year** supports lifecycle, career, property, leadership and generational change.
- **Era** groups historically meaningful structural periods for memory and archival compression.

Era is not a fixed-duration tick.

## 3. Fast-forward scope

The player may request any meaningful span, including:

- hours;
- days;
- weeks;
- months;
- seasons;
- years;
- multiple years;
- multiple decades.

There is no artificial rule requiring one-year confirmation steps.

## 4. Hierarchical resolution

A request to advance ten years must not execute ten years of full Day simulation.

Resolution should dynamically choose the coarsest safe level.

Low-event intervals may resolve at month/season/year scale.

High-event intervals expand into finer resolution when necessary.

## 5. Interrupt conditions

Fast-forward should stop early when a high-impact event reasonably requires player agency.

Potential interruption classes include:

- immediate threat to protagonist life, continuity or identity;
- major family/Tier A lifecycle events;
- dangerous Claim transitions;
- institutional seizure, schism, coup or direct action against the protagonist;
- major property/business loss or opportunity;
- irreversible case decisions;
- resurfacing long-term hooks;
- identity-exposure thresholds;
- bodily death/reconstruction;
- a player-authored alert condition.

The requested fast-forward duration is therefore a maximum target, not a promise to skip all intervening meaningful play.

## 6. Long-term stances

Before substantial fast-forward, the protagonist may define persistent stances.

Candidate domains:

- **Career:** maintain, expand, research, public office, retire/low activity.
- **Family:** close involvement, selective involvement, distance, focus on a specific person.
- **Occult:** conceal, investigate, deepen Claim, seek longevity, suppress anomalies.
- **Social:** low profile, network expansion, institutional influence.
- **Wealth:** preserve, invest, expand, liquidate.
- **Investigation:** normal cases, occult-only, delegated, inactive.

Stances guide compressed resolution and remain active until changed, invalidated or interrupted.

## 7. Unresolved matters

Active matters do not automatically freeze time.

The protagonist may:

- continue actively;
- abandon;
- delegate;
- defer;
- allow external actors/institutions to proceed.

The world may resolve, worsen, close, misclassify or transform those matters without the protagonist.

## 8. World activity during absence

The world continues during:

- voluntary fast-forward;
- travel/absence;
- bodily death;
- reconstruction;
- coma or incapacitation;
- long research periods.

The protagonist does not own the global clock.

## 9. Temporal compression

Long-term persistence should retain detail proportionally to relevance.

Recent/high-impact intervals keep rich state.

Older/lower-impact intervals may collapse into:

- monthly summaries;
- yearly summaries;
- era summaries;
- durable consequences;
- canonical facts;
- unresolved hooks.

Compression must preserve facts needed for later causal reasoning.
