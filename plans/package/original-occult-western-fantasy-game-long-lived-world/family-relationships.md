# Long-Lived World — Family and Relationships

**Task ID:** `refactor/original-occult-western-fantasy-long-lived-world`  
**Status:** Partially frozen / implementation not approved

## 1. Role of family

Family is a persistent world subsystem and a source of long-horizon consequences.

It is not a succession mechanism for player control. The same protagonist remains player-controlled for the whole save.

## 2. Supported bonds

The protagonist and relevant NPCs may participate in:

- romance and courtship;
- marriage;
- long-term partnership;
- separation and divorce where socially/legalistically applicable;
- widowhood;
- remarriage;
- biological parenthood;
- adoption;
- estrangement and reconciliation;
- hidden or disputed kinship;
- informal or secret partnerships.

Relationship structures are represented in the relation/family graph rather than hard-coded to a single household template.

## 3. Surface social norm

Public-facing society generally treats relatively traditional one-to-one partnership/marriage as the default social norm.

This is **not** a mechanical ban on other relationships.

Affairs, secret partners, informal unions, same-sex relationships, multi-partner structures and other arrangements may exist. Their consequences depend on contextual authority such as:

- era;
- jurisdiction/locality;
- social class;
- religion;
- institution;
- family reputation;
- public visibility.

The simulation should model consequences from world context rather than enforce a universal moral judgment.

## 4. Children and descendants

Children and important descendants age through real lifecycle stages.

They may become Tier A/B actors if their relevance warrants it.

A multi-generation family graph may persist across decades or centuries, but detailed simulation follows relevance. Unimportant branches may be compacted.

## 5. Lifecycle abstraction

The game should not become a repetitive childcare simulator.

Ordinary uneventful time may be abstracted.

High-impact milestones should be represented when relevant, including:

- birth/adoption;
- severe illness or loss;
- education and formative choices;
- discovery of the protagonist's abnormal longevity;
- occupation and institutional entry;
- romance/marriage;
- estrangement/reconciliation;
- inheritance;
- occult involvement;
- major conflict;
- death.

## 6. Intergenerational inheritance

Where world logic permits, later generations may inherit or receive:

- property and businesses;
- heirlooms and archives;
- family reputation;
- social obligations;
- favors and grudges;
- secrets;
- institutional relationships;
- unresolved cases;
- selected Claims or supernatural liabilities.

Inheritance must be causal and authority-validated.

## 7. Longevity and loved ones

The protagonist's longevity does not automatically extend to loved ones.

Spouses, partners, children and descendants age normally unless they separately undergo a valid longevity path.

Extending longevity to another person must be:

- intentional;
- setting-consistent;
- rare;
- costly;
- narratively significant;
- subject to the other person's agency and possible refusal.

The game explicitly permits the protagonist to outlive loved ones and multiple later generations.

## 8. Identity exposure through family history

Families can preserve evidence across generations.

Examples include:

- photographs;
- letters;
- legal records;
- portraits;
- inherited testimony;
- family stories;
- institutional archives.

Long-lived family contact therefore contributes to identity exposure and can reveal that the protagonist has not aged.
