### 6.26 Approved core gameplay loop and failure model — Round 5

The game is not a chat transcript with occasional variable updates and not a conventional quest / skill-check / combat RPG.

The approved core loop is:

**Acquire → Interpret → Commit → Consequence → Continue**

The player acquires information, interprets competing explanations, commits to an action that changes world state, accepts consequences, and continues in a world that keeps moving.

#### 6.26.1 Action categories

Ordinary player actions should resolve into authoritative world actions such as:

- **Observe** — inspect scenes, bodies, documents and environments;
- **Verify** — cross-check identity, time, ownership, records and testimony;
- **Interview** — question, negotiate, persuade, confront or pressure;
- **Access** — request, earn, borrow or illegally obtain institutional access;
- **Test** — experiment with a hypothesis or abnormal rule;
- **Act** — protect, arrest, remove, expose, destroy, rescue or physically intervene;
- **Invoke** — use a formal Claim;
- **Publish** — report, register, accuse, litigate or broadcast a version of events.

Natural-language input may express intent, but resolved game state must record what actually happened.

#### 6.26.2 Investigation information types

Investigation distinguishes at least:

**Evidence**

An actual acquired object, record, trace or observation.

**Testimony**

A claim made by a person or institution. It belongs to that source's Belief unless independently established.

**Finding**

A conclusion sufficiently verified through valid methods.

**Hypothesis**

A current explanatory model proposed by the player or an NPC.

These categories must not collapse into one generic “clue” list.

NPC testimony does not automatically become World Truth.

#### 6.26.3 Core information should not be hidden behind meaningless failure

If the protagonist has:

- a valid method;
- sufficient access;
- enough time;
- no meaningful opposing force,

then obtaining core information should normally succeed.

Do not insert arbitrary investigation checks merely to slow progress.

Uncertainty resolution is used only where:

- outcome is genuinely uncertain;
- meaningful opposition exists;
- time / exposure / danger matters;
- failure can create a new situation.

#### 6.26.4 Failure must create state

Preferred resolution outcomes are:

- **Clean** — objective achieved without meaningful additional cost;
- **Costly** — objective achieved with time, money, Exposure, relationship or other cost;
- **Complicated** — objective partly or substantially achieved while creating a new problem;
- **Denied** — this method cannot currently achieve the objective, but the attempt produces new state, information or opposition.

Denied must not normally mean “nothing happens; repeat the same input.”

#### 6.26.5 Capability, Position and Preparation

The game should not rely on a universal Investigation skill or large numeric skill ladder.

Action resolution depends primarily on:

**Capability**

Does the protagonist actually know how or possess a valid Claim / method to attempt this?

**Position**

Are current circumstances favorable: permission, access, trust, equipment, physical position, jurisdiction?

**Preparation**

What prior work has created leverage: research, credentials, maps, witnesses, tools, evidence, planning?

Character progression therefore expands methods and improves situations rather than merely inflating one universal number.

#### 6.26.6 Time as a major resource

Investigation consumes narrative time.

Choosing one lead may allow another situation to change.

Possible consequences of delay include:

- witnesses leaving;
- evidence decaying;
- press publishing first;
- institutional Agendas advancing;
- suspects acting;
- business hours ending;
- family or Personal Anchor commitments being missed;
- transport opportunities closing.

The central resource question is often:

**what deserves attention now?**

#### 6.26.7 Time granularity

Do not simulate every minute.

Use meaningful narrative durations such as:

- minutes for immediate actions;
- roughly an hour for meetings or focused visits;
- half-days for district travel plus investigation;
- days for substantial research or recovery;
- explicit train departures or opening hours when materially relevant.

Time exists to create consequence, not calendar micromanagement.

#### 6.26.8 Case failure does not equal game failure

A case may conclude badly while the campaign continues.

Examples:

- the wrong person is convicted;
- a suspect escapes;
- a mistaken Settlement becomes official;
- premature reporting stabilizes a false Public Belief;
- a person is saved but critical evidence is destroyed;
- Truth is found but no institution accepts it.

Such outcomes become persistent history.

The game should not assume reload as the normal response to an imperfect resolution.

#### 6.26.9 Combat role

Combat is a high-risk form of world interaction, not the default content loop.

Ordinary weapons remain dangerous.

A firearm, knife, fire or multiple ordinary attackers can seriously harm a supernatural practitioner.

Most mysteries should not end with “reveal monster, enter combat, kill it.”

Violence is one possible method among:

- retreat;
- negotiation;
- containment;
- calling authorities;
- changing physical position;
- using evidence as leverage;
- using a Claim to alter the situation.

#### 6.26.10 Injury model

The preferred physical harm model is **Condition / Injury**, not large regenerating HP pools.

Examples:

- bruising;
- blood loss;
- fracture;
- gunshot wound;
- concussion;
- burn;
- infection risk.

Injuries can affect:

- feasible actions;
- recovery time;
- medical cost;
- social obligations;
- Form-related supernatural interactions.

Rest does not trivially erase severe harm.

#### 6.26.11 Claims solve rule problems

Claims should not replace ordinary competence.

Mundane actions such as:

- shooting;
- following a person;
- reading;
- lying;
- ordinary first aid;
- interviewing

do not require supernatural buttons.

Claims become most valuable where ordinary reality is insufficient, unstable or being actively altered.

This preserves technology, profession and human competence.

#### 6.26.12 Downtime

Cases are separated or interrupted by meaningful ordinary life.

Downtime may be used for:

- physical recovery;
- professional paid work;
- spending time with Personal Anchors;
- Claim study;
- fulfilling Obligation;
- research;
- maintaining the office;
- relationship development;
- worship or community participation;
- debt and legal matters;
- following long-term leads.

Downtime is part of progression, not filler.

#### 6.26.13 Maintaining ordinary Identity

Personal Anchors require lived continuity.

The protagonist may need to:

- keep promises;
- visit family;
- maintain profession;
- return home;
- participate in familiar routines;
- remain present in community relationships.

These actions help preserve ordinary Identity against supernatural Displacement.

Do not reduce this to a single “Humanity +5” button.

The relevant relationships and routines remain concrete world state.

#### 6.26.14 Money

Money matters where it changes practical options:

- rent;
- travel;
- medical care;
- archive fees;
- professional license;
- office operation;
- equipment;
- legal assistance;
- hired help;
- bribery;
- debt.

Do not simulate trivial daily consumption for its own sake.

Economic pressure should redirect the player toward different social solutions rather than demand repetitive money grinding.

#### 6.26.15 Three time scales

Gameplay operates across three nested time scales.

**Scene**

Minutes to hours. Conversation, inspection, immediate conflict, Claim use.

**Day**

Travel, institutional visits, work, appointments, case prioritization.

**Arc**

Days to weeks. Institutional Agendas, political reform, relationships, major cases and long-term consequences.

NPCs and institutions need not be simulated second-by-second for these scales to remain meaningful.

#### 6.26.16 Missable information

The player may miss individual evidence, testimony or interpretations.

Critical progression must not depend on one fragile clue.

Multiple routes should support important conclusions.

However, missing evidence may change:

- confidence;
- available resolutions;
- which institution believes the player;
- costs;
- collateral damage;
- later consequences.

The goal is resilient investigation, not guaranteed perfect knowledge.

### 6.27 Round 5 decision

Approved:

- Acquire → Interpret → Commit → Consequence → Continue loop;
- authoritative action categories;
- Evidence / Testimony / Finding / Hypothesis separation;
- no arbitrary checks for information already obtainable by method and access;
- Clean / Costly / Complicated / Denied consequence model;
- Capability / Position / Preparation as core action-resolution inputs;
- time as a major strategic resource;
- narrative rather than minute-by-minute time;
- persistent bad case outcomes rather than automatic Game Over;
- low-frequency, high-risk violence;
- Condition / Injury instead of HP-sponging;
- Claims supplement rather than replace mundane competence;
- downtime as recovery, relationship and Identity-maintenance play;
- concrete Personal Anchor maintenance rather than one Humanity meter;
- meaningful but non-grindy money;
- Scene / Day / Arc time scales;
- missable evidence without single-clue dead ends.

Rejected:

- generic investigation-skill inflation;
- routine “roll to notice the required clue” design;
- failure as repeated no-state retries;
- combat as the default resolution;
- sleep-to-full-health severe injury recovery;
- every mystery ending in monster combat;
- exhaustive minute-level life simulation;
- one fragile required clue for core progression.

### 6.28 Round 5.5 question — deterministic checks and risk resolution

Round 5.5 must determine exactly how uncertain actions are resolved.

It must answer:

- whether any random roll is used;
- how Capability / Position / Preparation become deterministic inputs;
- whether the player sees risk before committing;
- how opposition and environmental difficulty are represented;
- how Clean / Costly / Complicated / Denied are selected;
- how Claims modify possibility without becoming automatic success;
- how injuries, stress, obligations and institutional support affect action resolution;
- how social conflict avoids becoming one generic persuasion score;
- how combat uses the same world-action model;
- which part of resolution belongs to Native authority and which part belongs to model narration;
- how to prevent the language model from arbitrarily deciding success because a response “sounds persuasive.”
