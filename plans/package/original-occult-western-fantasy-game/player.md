### 6.20 Approved player identity and opening structure — Round 4

The player begins as an ordinary licensed professional rather than a predestined supernatural figure.

#### 6.20.1 Fixed present role — independent Civil Verifier

The starting profession is provisionally an **independent Civil Verifier**: a licensed civilian specialist who resolves inconsistencies in modern identity and civil records.

Typical work includes:

- death-record conflict;
- insurance identity verification;
- inheritance claims;
- duplicate registration;
- broken title chains;
- marriage-status conflict;
- migrant name mismatch;
- worker-roll discrepancies;
- determining whether a claimant is legally the person they claim to be.

The exact setting-native profession title remains open.

#### 6.20.2 Why this role is preferred

The profession gives the player limited but plausible access to:

- police;
- courts;
- hospitals;
- registries;
- insurers;
- archives;
- witnesses;
- Church records in ordinary cases.

The player can knock on many doors without automatically belonging to the institution behind them.

This preserves role-playing freedom.

#### 6.20.3 Institutional independence

The player does not begin as a member of State, Church, Academy, major Capital or Grey Registry.

They may hold a municipal or professional license, but work independently or through a small practice / contract network.

Later social Identity may change radically.

Possible later directions include:

- official abnormal consultant;
- academic collaborator;
- journalist;
- clergy;
- corporate investigator;
- underground identity specialist;
- independent occult practitioner;
- founder of a new institution.

The starting profession is an entry point, not a permanent class.

#### 6.20.4 Fixed present, flexible past

The game fixes the player's current professional status but leaves most prior biography open.

Flexible background areas include:

- name and personal identity details;
- origin;
- family;
- prior training;
- faith;
- personal relationships;
- long-term motives;
- temperament.

Background must affect knowledge, relationships and Anchors rather than function only as flavor text.

#### 6.20.5 No chosen-one premise

The player does not begin as:

- secret divine heir;
- ancient reincarnation;
- unique bloodline;
- hidden god;
- only person capable of seeing Truth;
- predestined savior.

The player becomes exceptional through events, choices and the Identity they construct.

#### 6.20.6 Opening case — second death

The opening case begins as an apparently mundane identity problem.

A recently dead person possesses valid current identity evidence, but official records state that the same person died in the Eastbank Settlement approximately forty years earlier.

Initial mundane explanations remain plausible:

- fraud;
- stolen identity;
- clerical error;
- duplicate name;
- insurance crime;
- migration / registration mismatch.

The case reaches the player because identity verification is their ordinary work.

#### 6.20.7 Escalation

Investigation establishes that the recently deceased person did not merely adopt the identity recently.

The identity has decades of coherent continuity:

- tax or employment history;
- residence;
- family;
- marriage;
- neighbors;
- ordinary institutional records.

The central question changes from:

**who stole a dead person's identity?**

to:

**who, then, died forty years ago?**

#### 6.20.8 First Breach

The player's first Breach occurs because they preserve two mutually incompatible but strongly evidenced realities instead of allowing one to erase the other.

The player's professional habits matter:

- independent notes;
- evidence separation;
- repeated verification;
- direct Witness;
- refusal to collapse contradiction prematurely.

The player becomes a third-party Anchor capable of holding both versions long enough for ordinary settlement to fail.

The Breach is caused by action and circumstance, not innate destiny.

#### 6.20.9 Breach does not grant an automatic class

After the Breach, the player is in an unstable but meaningful state provisionally described as **Unsettled / Breached-but-uninvested**.

Consequences may include:

- increased sensitivity to contradiction;
- greater susceptibility to abnormal structures;
- potential compatibility with formal Claim establishment.

The Breach does not automatically grant:

- a class;
- a Pathway;
- a Principle;
- a fixed spell;
- an unavoidable Investiture.

#### 6.20.10 First Claim emerges from play

The first Claim should not be selected from a class menu.

The opening investigation creates plausible Claim directions based on what the player actually protected, pursued or prioritized.

Illustrative tendencies:

- preserving conflicting evidence → Witness / Memory;
- defending identity and family continuity → Name / Bond;
- pursuing obsolete streets and impossible spatial records → Boundary / Echo.

These are examples, not a frozen reward table.

The player later chooses how to stabilize the Breach through institutional Investiture, another tradition, or self-directed risk.

#### 6.20.11 Investiture may be refused

The player may temporarily refuse formal Investiture.

Benefits:

- no immediate formal Price;
- greater institutional independence;
- delayed supernatural self-definition.

Costs:

- instability;
- fewer supernatural tools;
- institutional suspicion;
- greater exposure to unresolved contradiction.

This makes the first supernatural commitment a story decision rather than character-creation bookkeeping.

#### 6.20.12 Starting access

Guaranteed:

- professional verification license;
- small practice / work-source network;
- ordinary civil-record access;
- limited police / court / registry contact.

Background-dependent:

- clergy contact;
- journalist contact;
- academic contact;
- labor contact;
- rail / industrial contact;
- insurer contact.

Must be earned:

- specialist abnormal-investigation access;
- restricted Church archive;
- restricted University research;
- core Capital records;
- deep Grey Registry access.

#### 6.20.13 Economic position

The player is not wealthy by default.

Ordinary costs may matter when they alter available social solutions:

- rent;
- transport;
- archive fees;
- legal help;
- professional insurance;
- office costs;
- assistants;
- equipment.

The game should not become a survival-economy simulator.

Money primarily changes which social routes are practical.

#### 6.20.14 Core thematic arc

The player's starting profession is to determine **who other people are**.

The long-term supernatural arc increasingly asks:

**who are you, and what still makes you the same person?**

This mirror between Civil Verification and personal Identity is approved as a central character theme.

### 6.21 Round 4 decision

Approved:

- independent Civil Verifier as the fixed starting profession;
- institutional independence at game start;
- fixed present / flexible past character design;
- no chosen-one origin;
- second-death identity case as opening;
- first Breach caused by holding contradictory evidence;
- Breach without automatic class or Claim;
- first Claim emerging from play and later stabilization choice;
- ability to postpone Investiture;
- tiered starting institutional access;
- modest economic life without survival-sim focus;
- verification-of-others → definition-of-self as the protagonist's core thematic arc.

Rejected:

- starting as a trained abnormal investigator;
- starting inside one major faction;
- fully blank protagonist with no social function;
- supernatural bloodline / predestination;
- class selection at character creation;
- automatic supernatural skill immediately upon first Breach.

### 6.22 Round 4.5 question — character creation, background Anchors and controlled freedom

Round 4.5 must determine:

- which player facts are fully freeform;
- which facts use curated categories;
- which background choices create formal Anchors;
- how many important starting relationships are allowed;
- how family, faith, class and prior profession affect institutional access;
- whether custom free-text background is permitted and how it is prevented from rewriting world canon;
- which starting NPC relationships are authored by the game versus generated from player choices;
- how to avoid a huge combinatorial opening burden;
- how to preserve meaningful replay variation without turning character creation into a spreadsheet.
