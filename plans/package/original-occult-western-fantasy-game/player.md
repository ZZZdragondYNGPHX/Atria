### 6.20 Approved player identity and opening structure — Round 4

The player begins as an ordinary licensed professional rather than a predestined supernatural figure.

#### 6.20.1 Fixed present role — independent Civil Verifier

The starting profession is provisionally an **independent Civil Verifier**: a licensed civilian specialist who resolves inconsistencies in modern identity and civil records.

Typical work includes:

- death-record conflict;
- insurance identity verification;
- inheritance claims;
- duplicate registration;
- broken title chains;
- marriage-status conflict;
- migrant name mismatch;
- worker-roll discrepancies;
- determining whether a claimant is legally the person they claim to be.

The exact setting-native profession title remains open.

#### 6.20.2 Why this role is preferred

The profession gives the player limited but plausible access to:

- police;
- courts;
- hospitals;
- registries;
- insurers;
- archives;
- witnesses;
- Church records in ordinary cases.

The player can knock on many doors without automatically belonging to the institution behind them.

This preserves role-playing freedom.

#### 6.20.3 Institutional independence

The player does not begin as a member of State, Church, Academy, major Capital or Grey Registry.

They may hold a municipal or professional license, but work independently or through a small practice / contract network.

Later social Identity may change radically.

Possible later directions include:

- official abnormal consultant;
- academic collaborator;
- journalist;
- clergy;
- corporate investigator;
- underground identity specialist;
- independent occult practitioner;
- founder of a new institution.

The starting profession is an entry point, not a permanent class.

#### 6.20.4 Fixed present, flexible past

The game fixes the player's current professional status but leaves most prior biography open.

Flexible background areas include:

- name and personal identity details;
- origin;
- family;
- prior training;
- faith;
- personal relationships;
- long-term motives;
- temperament.

Background must affect knowledge, relationships and Anchors rather than function only as flavor text.

#### 6.20.5 No chosen-one premise

The player does not begin as:

- secret divine heir;
- ancient reincarnation;
- unique bloodline;
- hidden god;
- only person capable of seeing Truth;
- predestined savior.

The player becomes exceptional through events, choices and the Identity they construct.

#### 6.20.6 Opening case — second death

The opening case begins as an apparently mundane identity problem.

A recently dead person possesses valid current identity evidence, but official records state that the same person died in the Eastbank Settlement approximately forty years earlier.

Initial mundane explanations remain plausible:

- fraud;
- stolen identity;
- clerical error;
- duplicate name;
- insurance crime;
- migration / registration mismatch.

The case reaches the player because identity verification is their ordinary work.

#### 6.20.7 Escalation

Investigation establishes that the recently deceased person did not merely adopt the identity recently.

The identity has decades of coherent continuity:

- tax or employment history;
- residence;
- family;
- marriage;
- neighbors;
- ordinary institutional records.

The central question changes from:

**who stole a dead person's identity?**

to:

**who, then, died forty years ago?**

#### 6.20.8 First Breach

The player's first Breach occurs because they preserve two mutually incompatible but strongly evidenced realities instead of allowing one to erase the other.

The player's professional habits matter:

- independent notes;
- evidence separation;
- repeated verification;
- direct Witness;
- refusal to collapse contradiction prematurely.

The player becomes a third-party Anchor capable of holding both versions long enough for ordinary settlement to fail.

The Breach is caused by action and circumstance, not innate destiny.

#### 6.20.9 Breach does not grant an automatic class

After the Breach, the player is in an unstable but meaningful state provisionally described as **Unsettled / Breached-but-uninvested**.

Consequences may include:

- increased sensitivity to contradiction;
- greater susceptibility to abnormal structures;
- potential compatibility with formal Claim establishment.

The Breach does not automatically grant:

- a class;
- a Pathway;
- a Principle;
- a fixed spell;
- an unavoidable Investiture.

#### 6.20.10 First Claim emerges from play

The first Claim should not be selected from a class menu.

The opening investigation creates plausible Claim directions based on what the player actually protected, pursued or prioritized.

Illustrative tendencies:

- preserving conflicting evidence → Witness / Memory;
- defending identity and family continuity → Name / Bond;
- pursuing obsolete streets and impossible spatial records → Boundary / Echo.

These are examples, not a frozen reward table.

The player later chooses how to stabilize the Breach through institutional Investiture, another tradition, or self-directed risk.

#### 6.20.11 Investiture may be refused

The player may temporarily refuse formal Investiture.

Benefits:

- no immediate formal Price;
- greater institutional independence;
- delayed supernatural self-definition.

Costs:

- instability;
- fewer supernatural tools;
- institutional suspicion;
- greater exposure to unresolved contradiction.

This makes the first supernatural commitment a story decision rather than character-creation bookkeeping.

#### 6.20.12 Starting access

Guaranteed:

- professional verification license;
- small practice / work-source network;
- ordinary civil-record access;
- limited police / court / registry contact.

Background-dependent:

- clergy contact;
- journalist contact;
- academic contact;
- labor contact;
- rail / industrial contact;
- insurer contact.

Must be earned:

- specialist abnormal-investigation access;
- restricted Church archive;
- restricted University research;
- core Capital records;
- deep Grey Registry access.

#### 6.20.13 Economic position

The player is not wealthy by default.

Ordinary costs may matter when they alter available social solutions:

- rent;
- transport;
- archive fees;
- legal help;
- professional insurance;
- office costs;
- assistants;
- equipment.

The game should not become a survival-economy simulator.

Money primarily changes which social routes are practical.

#### 6.20.14 Core thematic arc

The player's starting profession is to determine **who other people are**.

The long-term supernatural arc increasingly asks:

**who are you, and what still makes you the same person?**

This mirror between Civil Verification and personal Identity is approved as a central character theme.

### 6.21 Round 4 decision

Approved:

- independent Civil Verifier as the fixed starting profession;
- institutional independence at game start;
- fixed present / flexible past character design;
- no chosen-one origin;
- second-death identity case as opening;
- first Breach caused by holding contradictory evidence;
- Breach without automatic class or Claim;
- first Claim emerging from play and later stabilization choice;
- ability to postpone Investiture;
- tiered starting institutional access;
- modest economic life without survival-sim focus;
- verification-of-others → definition-of-self as the protagonist's core thematic arc.

Rejected:

- starting as a trained abnormal investigator;
- starting inside one major faction;
- fully blank protagonist with no social function;
- supernatural bloodline / predestination;
- class selection at character creation;
- automatic supernatural skill immediately upon first Breach.

### 6.22 Approved character creation and background Anchors — Round 4.5

Character creation uses a **controlled skeleton plus limited free expression**.

The player should feel that they authored the protagonist without being able to rewrite setting canon through biography text.

#### 6.22.1 Six-step character creation

The default flow is:

1. **Identity** — name, pronouns / gender presentation, adult age, appearance and other personal descriptors.
2. **Origin** — social and geographic upbringing.
3. **Prior Life** — training or work before becoming a Civil Verifier.
4. **Faith** — relationship to the dominant religious world and local tradition.
5. **Anchor** — one important person and optionally one important place / community.
6. **Reason** — why the protagonist currently performs Civil Verification and what ordinary life goal matters now.

Do not add class, Principle, supernatural profession, talent-point or stat-allocation selection at character creation.

#### 6.22.2 Fully free personal expression

The following may be entered freely within ordinary validation limits:

- name;
- pronouns / gender presentation;
- adult age;
- appearance;
- mannerisms;
- personality notes;
- preferences;
- ordinary personal motives.

These fields define the protagonist rather than external world authority.

#### 6.22.3 Structured world-impact background

Background choices that alter world access or facts must use curated setting-valid categories.

Initial structured areas include:

**Origin**

Examples:

- Old Quarter urban family;
- industrial worker household;
- middle-class clerical household;
- surrounding county / town;
- migrant household;
- declining minor old family;
- Church-charity upbringing.

**Prior Life**

Examples:

- legal / clerical work;
- accounting / insurance;
- medical support;
- Church schooling;
- journalism;
- railway / industrial work;
- military service;
- ordinary administration.

**Faith**

Examples:

- ordinary member of the ecumenical tradition;
- nominal / culturally affiliated;
- devout;
- local-tradition background;
- convert;
- distanced / nonbelieving;
- unresolved dual religious identity.

These are working categories, not final UI wording.

#### 6.22.4 Background rewards are access, not stat bonuses

Background should affect:

- **Knowledge Access** — what the protagonist already understands;
- **Social Access** — who will initially speak to or recognize them;
- **Procedural Familiarity** — which documents, routines and institutions they can interpret;
- **Starting Belief** — which ordinary assumptions the protagonist plausibly brings into play;
- **Relationships** — a limited number of credible initial contacts.

Do not reduce these backgrounds to generic numeric bonuses such as “Investigation +2”.

A railway background may recognize obsolete route numbering; a Church-school background may recognize an old burial notation; an insurance background may understand Settlement claim codes.

Every route must provide additional interpretation, not a mandatory solution gate.

#### 6.22.5 Personal Anchor requirement

The protagonist begins with at least **one living important person** who functions as a real Personal Anchor.

Permitted relationship types may include:

- parent or guardian;
- sibling;
- spouse;
- lover;
- close friend;
- mentor;
- former colleague;
- long-term business partner.

The player may provide:

- relationship type;
- name;
- one or two short descriptive facts.

The system then instantiates that person as a stable Actor consistent with world canon.

This NPC represents part of the protagonist's pre-supernatural ordinary Identity and can later make Displacement emotionally and mechanically legible.

#### 6.22.6 Optional place / community Anchor

The player may also choose at most one important non-personal Anchor such as:

- home;
- office;
- neighborhood;
- parish;
- mutual-aid society;
- school;
- café;
- community.

This creates a second form of ordinary continuity without overloading the starting state.

#### 6.22.7 Limit starting NPC proliferation

Character creation should instantiate only **1–2 important Anchor NPCs**.

Other family or history facts may exist as compact background facts until they become relevant.

Example:

“the protagonist has two younger brothers living in the countryside” may remain one background fact rather than two fully simulated Actors until the story needs them.

This prevents unnecessary memory and simulation load.

#### 6.22.8 Canon-safe free-text rule

Approved rule:

**the player may define their own past, but may not use biography text to define major world canon.**

Allowed examples:

- ordinary parental occupation;
- ordinary childhood event;
- interpersonal history;
- ordinary education;
- personal fear or preference.

Disallowed as authoritative character-creation facts:

- secret royal succession;
- ownership of a major national institution;
- possession of an unknown ninth Principle;
- prior confirmed meeting with the highest divine truth;
- already possessing forbidden high-level Claims;
- rewriting the established history of Eastbank or major institutions.

Out-of-bounds text should be rejected or reframed as a personal belief / rumor only when the player explicitly wants that uncertainty.

#### 6.22.9 Do not retroactively supernaturalize ordinary biography

The game must not automatically reinterpret ordinary player-authored memories as hidden cosmic events.

If the player writes that funeral bells frightened them after a parent's death, this remains an ordinary formative memory unless later story evidence independently creates a supernatural connection.

This protects player authorship and preserves the impact of later supernatural intrusion.

#### 6.22.10 Starting Actors become authoritative after creation

Once generated from the approved background:

- important NPC identity;
- profession;
- location;
- relationship;
- major history

become authoritative world state.

The model may portray them but cannot casually change these facts between scenes.

#### 6.22.11 Background must appear immediately in play

The opening case should expose at least one background-specific interpretation or access path early.

Examples:

- railway familiarity detects obsolete line notation;
- Church education recognizes old burial shorthand;
- insurance experience recognizes Settlement claims coding;
- journalism experience recognizes publication or editorial conventions.

These are alternate informative routes rather than required keys.

#### 6.22.12 Replay principle

Background variation should change:

- what is noticed first;
- who is initially reachable;
- which explanations feel plausible;
- what evidence is easy or costly to obtain;
- which early institutional route opens naturally.

It should not require completely separate opening campaigns for every background.

### 6.23 Round 4.5 decision

Approved:

- six-step character creation;
- free self-expression for personal details;
- curated Origin / Prior Life / Faith categories for world-impact choices;
- one required living Personal Anchor;
- optional one Place / Community Anchor;
- only 1–2 important starting Anchor NPCs instantiated;
- background rewards as Knowledge / Access / Familiarity / Relationships;
- canon-safe free-text boundary;
- no automatic supernatural reinterpretation of biography;
- starting Actors become authoritative after creation;
- early background-specific observations without hard route gating.

Rejected:

- unrestricted biography text as world authority;
- large family-tree simulation at character creation;
- stat-point or class optimization during character creation;
- backgrounds that are only cosmetic;
- background-exclusive solutions required to complete the opening case.

### 6.24 Round 4.6 question — first Claim candidate generation and stabilization

Round 4.6 must define how opening play produces the protagonist's first legitimate Claim without allowing the language model to invent unbounded abilities.

It must determine:

- what player actions are tracked before the Breach;
- how those actions produce Principle tendencies;
- whether tendencies use hidden scores, discrete evidence tags or another deterministic representation;
- how many Claim candidates are surfaced;
- how candidate Claims are authored and validated;
- how Condition / Effect / Anchor / Price / Jurisdiction are constrained for a first Claim;
- how different institutions can offer different stabilization methods for the same underlying candidate;
- whether self-Investiture is possible immediately or only later;
- how refusal of Investiture remains playable;
- how to prevent optimization-by-dialogue where the player simply asks the model for an overpowered Claim.
