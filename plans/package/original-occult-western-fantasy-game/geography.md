### 6.17 Approved political geography and primary stage — Round 3.7

The initial game world is geographically focused but politically connected.

### 6.17.1 Primary state

The player begins in a **modernizing constitutional monarchy** where old and new Anchor systems coexist uneasily.

Core features:

- monarch and Crown remain meaningful;
- hereditary nobility still matters;
- parliament possesses real political power;
- bureaucracy is rapidly expanding;
- industrial Capital increasingly influences policy;
- Church jurisdiction is being reduced in some civil domains;
- Academy institutions are becoming politically important.

The state represents a negotiated coexistence between inherited authority and modern administrative reality.

### 6.17.2 King versus Crown

The monarch as person and the **Crown as Office Identity** are distinct.

The Crown is stabilized through:

- succession law;
- coronation;
- noble recognition;
- church rite;
- bureaucracy;
- military oath;
- state archives;
- territorial continuity.

A monarch therefore gains extraordinary Office Claims through legitimate Bond with the Crown rather than through magical blood by default.

### 6.17.3 Succession contradiction

Competing legitimate claimants can create a **sovereign contradiction** if different parts of the Anchor network recognize incompatible holders of the Crown.

Possible consequences include:

- conflicting legal authority;
- regional failure of Office Claims;
- unstable land and title Bonds;
- contradictory military or clerical loyalty;
- long-lasting Echo after civil war.

Past succession conflict is therefore a plausible source of present-day anomalies.

### 6.17.4 Primary city type

The initial playable city is **not the national capital**.

It is the state's second great city: a large industrial, port and railway metropolis combining:

- major harbor;
- rail junction;
- heavy industry;
- finance and insurance;
- university;
- printing and newspapers;
- immigration;
- ancient religious and civic institutions;
- nearby aristocratic estates and rural districts.

This allows national institutions to matter without placing the player immediately at the political center.

### 6.17.5 Why the second city is preferred

The city sits directly on the seam between:

- inherited urban institutions;
- industrial modernity;
- migration;
- financial Capital;
- university research;
- state expansion;
- Church tradition.

It can support long-form play while remaining locally coherent.

### 6.17.6 Urban reality gradient

The city and its surroundings exhibit a qualitative stability gradient.

**Administrative core**

Dense records, surveillance, synchronized time and institutional Witness. Abnormal events are rarer but more serious.

**Industrial / commercial districts**

Dense modern Anchors combined with rapid change and institutional contradiction. Abnormalities tend to be new, procedural or systemic.

**Peripheral districts and towns**

Modern administration overlaps with local tradition.

**Rural and remote areas**

Lower density of modern records and greater persistence of old local Anchors.

Travel away from the urban core should feel like reality becoming less administratively settled.

### 6.17.7 Six functional urban zones

The main city should contain at least these functional zone types:

1. **Old Quarter** — cathedral, old courts, aristocratic houses, historic archives.
2. **Civic District** — municipal offices, police, new courts, registry, post and telegraph.
3. **Exchange / Financial District** — banks, insurers, company offices, legal firms and securities.
4. **University Quarter** — university, laboratories, medical institutions, museums and academic presses.
5. **Industrial Belt** — rail, steel, mechanical works, electrical plants and worker districts.
6. **Docks** — warehouses, sailors, foreign communities, smuggling, migration and unstable identity.

Formal district names remain open.

### 6.17.8 Industrial Echo

Repeated industrial action may produce new forms of Echo.

Factories, rail yards and machines that perform highly repetitive processes can accumulate distinctive residual structures.

This creates a specifically industrial form of supernatural hazard distinct from ancient haunted sites.

### 6.17.9 External political contrasts

The initial geopolitical field needs only three major external contrasts.

#### Centralized Republic

Represents maximal modern **State / Civil Identity**.

Features may include:

- abolished or weakened hereditary privilege;
- unified national registry;
- standardized education;
- strong bureaucracy;
- secular or state-controlled civil institutions.

Strength: high reality stability.

Risk: suppressed local, ethnic or historical Identities may accumulate contradiction beneath official uniformity.

#### Multiethnic Dynastic Empire

Represents **Tradition / Dynasty / Local Anchor**.

Features may include:

- ancient ruling house;
- many peoples and legal traditions;
- regional aristocracy;
- several religious rites;
- incomplete administrative standardization.

It may appear administratively weaker while preserving powerful old Claim traditions that modern institutions cannot easily reproduce.

#### Maritime Commercial League / Republic

Represents **Capital / Contract / Corporate Identity**.

Features may include:

- merchant government;
- banks and insurers;
- chartered companies;
- overseas ports;
- transnational contracts;
- powerful corporate Identities.

Some companies may possess Anchor networks stronger than minor states.

### 6.17.10 Church across borders

The ecumenical communion crosses political borders.

This creates structural tension:

- states rely on Church rites and social institutions;
- states fear transnational clerical authority;
- national churches seek autonomy;
- disputes emerge over appointment, property, courts, education and registration.

### 6.17.11 National borders as modern Boundary infrastructure

Modern borders increasingly use:

- passport;
- customs;
- citizenship;
- rail inspection;
- maps;
- border posts;
- standardized legal identity.

State Boundary therefore becomes increasingly real.

Office Claims and legal recognition may weaken or change beyond recognized borders.

### 6.17.12 Migration and identity mismatch

Migration creates natural Name and Bond contradictions when different systems disagree over:

- spelling or transliteration;
- birth date;
- family relation;
- marriage;
- nationality;
- religious status;
- professional qualification.

Migrants are not inherently abnormal; incompatibility between identity systems creates the risk.

### 6.17.13 Colonial and overseas systems

Colonial expansion exists in the wider world but is **not a primary opening-game system**.

It may involve violent imposition of modern:

- Name;
- Boundary;
- property;
- registry;
- corporate Bond

over pre-existing local identity and sacred systems.

This is reserved for later arcs rather than used to over-expand the opening scope.

### 6.17.14 Initial geographic scope

The first implementation should model:

**one main city + surrounding counties / towns + one major railway corridor.**

The wider world remains present through:

- newspapers;
- migration;
- trade;
- diplomacy;
- foreign scholars;
- church networks;
- companies;
- telegrams.

This preserves world scale without requiring simultaneous simulation of an entire continent.

### 6.17.15 National reform background

The main state is approaching a major **Reality Consolidation** reform program.

Candidate reforms include:

- national identity system;
- mandatory birth and death registration;
- civil marriage;
- national standardized time;
- corporation law reform;
- church-school reform;
- standardized abnormal-incident reporting.

Supporters see safety and modernization.

Opponents may fear:

- excessive state supernatural authority;
- erosion of sacred Identity;
- destruction of local custom;
- over-stabilization of reality itself.

This reform wave is approved as a long-term political background.

### 6.17.16 Round 3.7 decision

Approved:

- constitutional-monarchy main state;
- Crown as Office Identity distinct from monarch;
- succession contradiction as a real supernatural hazard;
- second-city industrial port as main stage;
- six functional urban zones;
- industrial Echo as a modern anomaly class;
- centralized Republic, dynastic Empire and commercial League as external political contrasts;
- transnational Church;
- modern borders as Boundary infrastructure;
- migration identity mismatch;
- colonial systems present but deferred from opening scope;
- main city + surrounding region + railway corridor as initial simulation scope;
- Reality Consolidation reform as national background.

Rejected:

- opening in the national capital;
- immediate continent-wide free-roaming simulation;
- politics as decorative faction lore disconnected from Anchor mechanics;
- treating industrial modernity as merely aesthetic background.

### 6.18 Approved city history and first major secret — Round 3.8

The main city is a layered metropolis whose modern stability was built over an unresolved historical contradiction.

#### 6.18.1 Founding logic

The city began around a major river mouth, natural deep-water harbor and old crossing / ferry. Its earliest institutions handled passage, ownership and belonging, so the city's oldest social logic already touched Boundary, Bond and Name.

Its foundational question has always been:

**who may enter, who belongs, who leaves, and who owns what here?**

#### 6.18.2 Mercantile layer

Before industrialization, the city grew into a major port and trading center.

Older institutions include guilds, warehouses, early insurers, merchant courts, foreign trading houses, Church charities, noble estates, old cemeteries and inherited property systems.

The Old Quarter still preserves obsolete but partially effective Anchor structures. Some old rules, street names, property clauses and ritual practices remain because they still perform real stabilizing functions.

#### 6.18.3 Industrial layer

Railway expansion transformed the city into the major transfer point between maritime trade and inland transport.

This accelerated:

- population growth;
- industrial expansion;
- worker districts;
- banking and insurance;
- corporate headquarters;
- telegraphy;
- modern newspapers;
- university growth;
- annexation of surrounding settlements.

The modern city therefore contains three historical layers at once:

1. old boundary / mercantile city;
2. commercial nineteenth-century city;
3. industrial and administrative metropolis.

#### 6.18.4 The Eastbank Settlement

Approximately 35–45 years before game start, the city suffered a major disaster provisionally known as the **Eastbank Settlement**.

Official history describes a large fire, transport disruption, telegraph failure, mass casualties and reconstruction.

The deeper problem was not fire alone.

Eastbank was an early testbed for modern administrative identity systems:

- standardized addresses;
- property numbering;
- worker registries;
- residence records;
- railway time;
- telegraph reporting;
- insurance records.

These new systems overlapped with older Church registers, guild records, customary property, local street names, immigrant naming systems, informal residence and unregistered labor.

The disaster caused those systems to fail and contradict one another at scale.

#### 6.18.5 Identity and population contradiction

After the disaster, institutions could not agree on:

- how many people were present;
- who died;
- who survived;
- who legally owned destroyed property;
- which names referred to the same people;
- which addresses still existed;
- which debts and insurance claims remained valid.

Possible cases include official dead who later appeared elsewhere, living people with no valid birth or residence record, destroyed houses still remembered as occupied, and mutually exclusive identities.

The supernatural crisis was a failure of reality to settle who and what had actually existed in Eastbank.

#### 6.18.6 Settlement as emergency stabilization

The government did not fully recover Truth.

Instead, State, Church, Capital and Academy cooperated in a massive **Settlement** operation.

They established:

- an official death list;
- legal inheritance;
- valid insurance claims;
- surviving debt;
- recognized property ownership;
- accepted burial status;
- new street and district identities;
- new planning and construction.

This created a coherent enough Anchor network to stop the district from continuing to destabilize.

The intervention is not framed as a simple evil conspiracy. It may genuinely have prevented wider urban reality failure.

#### 6.18.7 Consistency rather than Truth

The key approved principle is:

**the Settlement stabilized one administratively coherent version of Eastbank without proving that version was the complete Truth.**

Institutional Belief became extremely strong while underlying contradiction remained.

The intervention both saved the city and excluded people, claims and histories that could not be incorporated.

#### 6.18.8 Modern district over unresolved history

The former disaster zone is now one of the city's most modern areas, especially the Exchange / financial district and major rail / transport infrastructure.

Modern contracts, transactions, timetables, public records and property systems continually reinforce the new city Identity.

The city's densest New Anchors therefore sit directly above its greatest unresolved historical contradiction.

#### 6.18.9 Persistent anomalies

Residual contradiction should manifest through identity and infrastructure rather than generic haunting.

Examples include:

- mail repeatedly routed to a street abolished decades ago;
- an old co-owner reappearing in every version of a modern property record;
- children describing routes through streets that no longer exist;
- fresh bodies whose legal death was recorded forty years earlier;
- insurance or pension obligations with no valid living claimant;
- addresses that exist only in certain record systems;
- official maps that subtly disagree after repeated copying.

These examples establish tone rather than an exhaustive encounter list.

#### 6.18.10 Fragmented institutional truth

No single institution possesses complete evidence.

Potential holdings:

- State archives — official Settlement narrative;
- Church — mismatched burial and parish registers;
- Academy — restricted anomaly studies;
- insurers — unusually complete demographic and property cross-records;
- noble estates — pre-industrial deeds and boundary descriptions;
- worker families — oral and household Memory;
- secret networks — identities intentionally excluded from Settlement.

Long-term investigation therefore requires crossing institutional boundaries.

#### 6.18.11 Truth restoration is not automatically benign

The game rejects a simple ending where discovering a definitive list automatically fixes Eastbank.

Four decades of later reality now depend on current property, families, institutions, contracts, infrastructure and civil status.

Restoring an older Truth may invalidate or destabilize present Bonds.

A central thematic question is therefore:

**should every buried truth be restored if the present has become real around the compromise?**

#### 6.18.12 First long-form mystery

The first city-scale mystery may begin with:

**a person legally recorded as dead in Eastbank forty years ago being found freshly dead again.**

Identity fraud is initially plausible.

The case can expand through old insurance records, photographs, obsolete addresses, contradictory maps and additional people who should not exist.

This moves the player from ordinary investigation into the city's buried institutional contradiction.

#### 6.18.13 City character

The city should have a strong collective self-image:

- practical;
- modern;
- rational;
- commercially proud;
- administratively competent;
- suspicious of superstition.

Its civic mythology is:

**we solve problems.**

Its deepest weakness is that its greatest historical problem was stabilized rather than truly resolved.

#### 6.18.14 District relationship to city Memory

The six functional districts represent different versions of the city:

- **Old Quarter** — pre-Settlement Memory and inherited Anchor;
- **Civic District** — official modern narrative and Civil Identity;
- **Exchange** — property and financial reality created after Settlement;
- **University Quarter** — restricted analytical knowledge;
- **Industrial Belt** — worker and descendant Memory plus Industrial Echo;
- **Docks** — continuous influx of unstable or cross-system identities.

#### 6.18.15 Deeper buried cause

A deeper explanation remains intentionally unresolved.

One approved possibility for later investigation is that Eastbank already sat over an ancient Boundary / Identity structure, and modern registration attempted to define too precisely a place that had historically remained partially ambiguous.

The administrative reform may therefore have triggered rather than created the underlying problem.

This thread is reserved for later revelation and should not be explained in the opening arc.

#### 6.18.16 Round 3.8 decision

Approved:

- city founded at a passage / port / boundary junction;
- mercantile and industrial historical layers;
- Eastbank disaster approximately four decades before game start;
- overlapping old and new identity systems as the core disaster mechanism;
- multi-institution Settlement as emergency stabilization;
- Settlement resolving consistency rather than full Truth;
- present financial / transport center built over unresolved Eastbank;
- identity / infrastructure anomalies rather than generic haunting;
- fragmented evidence across institutions;
- truth restoration carrying present-day costs;
- first long mystery beginning with the second death of an already-dead person;
- civic Identity of rational competence;
- possible deeper pre-modern Boundary cause reserved for later revelation.
