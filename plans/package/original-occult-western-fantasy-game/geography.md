### 6.17 Approved political geography and primary stage — Round 3.7

The initial game world is geographically focused but politically connected.

### 6.17.1 Primary state

The player begins in a **modernizing constitutional monarchy** where old and new Anchor systems coexist uneasily.

Core features:

- monarch and Crown remain meaningful;
- hereditary nobility still matters;
- parliament possesses real political power;
- bureaucracy is rapidly expanding;
- industrial Capital increasingly influences policy;
- Church jurisdiction is being reduced in some civil domains;
- Academy institutions are becoming politically important.

The state represents a negotiated coexistence between inherited authority and modern administrative reality.

### 6.17.2 King versus Crown

The monarch as person and the **Crown as Office Identity** are distinct.

The Crown is stabilized through:

- succession law;
- coronation;
- noble recognition;
- church rite;
- bureaucracy;
- military oath;
- state archives;
- territorial continuity.

A monarch therefore gains extraordinary Office Claims through legitimate Bond with the Crown rather than through magical blood by default.

### 6.17.3 Succession contradiction

Competing legitimate claimants can create a **sovereign contradiction** if different parts of the Anchor network recognize incompatible holders of the Crown.

Possible consequences include:

- conflicting legal authority;
- regional failure of Office Claims;
- unstable land and title Bonds;
- contradictory military or clerical loyalty;
- long-lasting Echo after civil war.

Past succession conflict is therefore a plausible source of present-day anomalies.

### 6.17.4 Primary city type

The initial playable city is **not the national capital**.

It is the state's second great city: a large industrial, port and railway metropolis combining:

- major harbor;
- rail junction;
- heavy industry;
- finance and insurance;
- university;
- printing and newspapers;
- immigration;
- ancient religious and civic institutions;
- nearby aristocratic estates and rural districts.

This allows national institutions to matter without placing the player immediately at the political center.

### 6.17.5 Why the second city is preferred

The city sits directly on the seam between:

- inherited urban institutions;
- industrial modernity;
- migration;
- financial Capital;
- university research;
- state expansion;
- Church tradition.

It can support long-form play while remaining locally coherent.

### 6.17.6 Urban reality gradient

The city and its surroundings exhibit a qualitative stability gradient.

**Administrative core**

Dense records, surveillance, synchronized time and institutional Witness. Abnormal events are rarer but more serious.

**Industrial / commercial districts**

Dense modern Anchors combined with rapid change and institutional contradiction. Abnormalities tend to be new, procedural or systemic.

**Peripheral districts and towns**

Modern administration overlaps with local tradition.

**Rural and remote areas**

Lower density of modern records and greater persistence of old local Anchors.

Travel away from the urban core should feel like reality becoming less administratively settled.

### 6.17.7 Six functional urban zones

The main city should contain at least these functional zone types:

1. **Old Quarter** — cathedral, old courts, aristocratic houses, historic archives.
2. **Civic District** — municipal offices, police, new courts, registry, post and telegraph.
3. **Exchange / Financial District** — banks, insurers, company offices, legal firms and securities.
4. **University Quarter** — university, laboratories, medical institutions, museums and academic presses.
5. **Industrial Belt** — rail, steel, mechanical works, electrical plants and worker districts.
6. **Docks** — warehouses, sailors, foreign communities, smuggling, migration and unstable identity.

Formal district names remain open.

### 6.17.8 Industrial Echo

Repeated industrial action may produce new forms of Echo.

Factories, rail yards and machines that perform highly repetitive processes can accumulate distinctive residual structures.

This creates a specifically industrial form of supernatural hazard distinct from ancient haunted sites.

### 6.17.9 External political contrasts

The initial geopolitical field needs only three major external contrasts.

#### Centralized Republic

Represents maximal modern **State / Civil Identity**.

Features may include:

- abolished or weakened hereditary privilege;
- unified national registry;
- standardized education;
- strong bureaucracy;
- secular or state-controlled civil institutions.

Strength: high reality stability.

Risk: suppressed local, ethnic or historical Identities may accumulate contradiction beneath official uniformity.

#### Multiethnic Dynastic Empire

Represents **Tradition / Dynasty / Local Anchor**.

Features may include:

- ancient ruling house;
- many peoples and legal traditions;
- regional aristocracy;
- several religious rites;
- incomplete administrative standardization.

It may appear administratively weaker while preserving powerful old Claim traditions that modern institutions cannot easily reproduce.

#### Maritime Commercial League / Republic

Represents **Capital / Contract / Corporate Identity**.

Features may include:

- merchant government;
- banks and insurers;
- chartered companies;
- overseas ports;
- transnational contracts;
- powerful corporate Identities.

Some companies may possess Anchor networks stronger than minor states.

### 6.17.10 Church across borders

The ecumenical communion crosses political borders.

This creates structural tension:

- states rely on Church rites and social institutions;
- states fear transnational clerical authority;
- national churches seek autonomy;
- disputes emerge over appointment, property, courts, education and registration.

### 6.17.11 National borders as modern Boundary infrastructure

Modern borders increasingly use:

- passport;
- customs;
- citizenship;
- rail inspection;
- maps;
- border posts;
- standardized legal identity.

State Boundary therefore becomes increasingly real.

Office Claims and legal recognition may weaken or change beyond recognized borders.

### 6.17.12 Migration and identity mismatch

Migration creates natural Name and Bond contradictions when different systems disagree over:

- spelling or transliteration;
- birth date;
- family relation;
- marriage;
- nationality;
- religious status;
- professional qualification.

Migrants are not inherently abnormal; incompatibility between identity systems creates the risk.

### 6.17.13 Colonial and overseas systems

Colonial expansion exists in the wider world but is **not a primary opening-game system**.

It may involve violent imposition of modern:

- Name;
- Boundary;
- property;
- registry;
- corporate Bond

over pre-existing local identity and sacred systems.

This is reserved for later arcs rather than used to over-expand the opening scope.

### 6.17.14 Initial geographic scope

The first implementation should model:

**one main city + surrounding counties / towns + one major railway corridor.**

The wider world remains present through:

- newspapers;
- migration;
- trade;
- diplomacy;
- foreign scholars;
- church networks;
- companies;
- telegrams.

This preserves world scale without requiring simultaneous simulation of an entire continent.

### 6.17.15 National reform background

The main state is approaching a major **Reality Consolidation** reform program.

Candidate reforms include:

- national identity system;
- mandatory birth and death registration;
- civil marriage;
- national standardized time;
- corporation law reform;
- church-school reform;
- standardized abnormal-incident reporting.

Supporters see safety and modernization.

Opponents may fear:

- excessive state supernatural authority;
- erosion of sacred Identity;
- destruction of local custom;
- over-stabilization of reality itself.

This reform wave is approved as a long-term political background.

### 6.17.16 Round 3.7 decision

Approved:

- constitutional-monarchy main state;
- Crown as Office Identity distinct from monarch;
- succession contradiction as a real supernatural hazard;
- second-city industrial port as main stage;
- six functional urban zones;
- industrial Echo as a modern anomaly class;
- centralized Republic, dynastic Empire and commercial League as external political contrasts;
- transnational Church;
- modern borders as Boundary infrastructure;
- migration identity mismatch;
- colonial systems present but deferred from opening scope;
- main city + surrounding region + railway corridor as initial simulation scope;
- Reality Consolidation reform as national background.

Rejected:

- opening in the national capital;
- immediate continent-wide free-roaming simulation;
- politics as decorative faction lore disconnected from Anchor mechanics;
- treating industrial modernity as merely aesthetic background.

### 6.18 Round 3.8 question — city history, structure and first major secret

Round 3.8 must define why the primary city exists in its present form and what historical contradictions remain embedded beneath it.

It must determine:

- the city's original founding logic;
- why it became a port / industrial / railway center;
- which older settlement or sacred structure existed before industrial expansion;
- what major disaster, uprising, fire, epidemic, collapse or war reshaped the city;
- which districts were rebuilt over older Anchor systems;
- whether one past event is officially remembered incorrectly;
- what first major city-scale mystery can connect ordinary crime, institutional history and supernatural contradiction;
- how much of the city's buried history should be accessible in the opening game;
- how to make the city itself feel like a character rather than a static hub.
