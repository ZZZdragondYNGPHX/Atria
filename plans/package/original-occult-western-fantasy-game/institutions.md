### 6.19 Approved city institutions and faction network — Round 3.9

The city's institutions form an interdependent operating system rather than a set of isolated reputation factions.

### 6.19.1 Ordinary city police

Most police are ordinary professionals handling ordinary crime, disorder, traffic, fire, missing persons, fraud and violence.

Basic abnormal-scene procedure includes:

- separate witnesses;
- establish and record time;
- preserve scene boundaries;
- verify identity;
- avoid casually moving unknown relics or contradictory records;
- escalate persistent Name / address / map / death-status contradictions.

Ordinary institutions remain relevant even when the supernatural is involved.

### 6.19.2 Specialist abnormal-investigation authority

The city has a public specialist authority for abnormal incidents.

Its core functions are:

- **Classification** — identify the type and scope of contradiction;
- **Stabilization** — prevent further spread or settlement failure;
- **Attribution** — distinguish accident, crime, Claim abuse, relic effect or institutional failure;
- **Resolution** — choose among restoring Truth, maintaining a Settlement, rebuilding Anchors or containing unresolved contradiction.

It is deliberately not omnipotent.

Searches, Church records, company books, dangerous experiments and sacred Identity cases require cooperation or legal authority from other institutions.

Its institutional specialty is coordination.

### 6.19.3 City university and applied occult research

The city's major university is a modern comprehensive institution with law, medicine, engineering, history, statistics, psychology / memory research and formal occult studies.

Its occult research body is provisionally an applied ontology / anomalous studies institute.

Its defining institutional advantage is willingness to systematize and publish.

This makes it both an engine of understanding and a source of risk.

Potential failures include:

- creating controlled Breaches that are not actually controlled;
- exploitative research on patients, workers or migrants;
- stripping local traditions into decontextualized data;
- publishing theories whose social adoption changes Anchor behavior.

The Academy is not the default moral faction.

### 6.19.4 Metropolitan Church structure

The city has a metropolitan Church hierarchy that operates:

- parishes;
- hospitals;
- schools;
- cemeteries;
- marriage and burial systems;
- charities;
- religious records.

A small specialist office handles:

- sacred relics;
- heresy;
- failed burial;
- sacred Office Claims;
- local sacred Identities;
- dangerous prayer / Address practices;
- religious Identity conflict.

This office frequently cooperates with the abnormal-investigation authority and frequently disputes its interpretation.

### 6.19.5 Insurance and banking conglomerate

One major private financial institution grew from maritime insurance into insurance, banking, investment, property and industrial finance.

Its significance to Eastbank is exceptional because the Settlement forced insurers to reconstruct:

- households;
- property;
- cargo;
- debt;
- mortality;
- dependents;
- claims.

As a result, its commercial archives may contain the city's most complete non-state cross-record of Eastbank.

Its historical role is intentionally morally mixed:

- it helped restore economic continuity;
- it compensated many survivors;
- it preserved otherwise lost evidence;
- it also denied people who could not prove identity;
- it benefited from settled property and unresolved claims.

### 6.19.6 Railway / port / heavy-industry corporation

A major transport-industrial corporation controls or strongly influences:

- rail;
- freight;
- port logistics;
- heavy industry;
- worker housing;
- timetables and transport records.

Rail infrastructure is a major modern Anchor network through repeated routes, times, stations, tickets, uniforms, offices and procedure.

The corporation is a prime candidate for emergent **Institutional Identity**.

A long-term risk is divergence between human management decisions and the stable operational Identity accumulated by the institution itself.

This is not framed as machine intelligence.

### 6.19.7 Old estate network

Old aristocratic interests are a network rather than one unified faction.

Their relevance comes from:

- historic land deeds;
- family archives;
- patronage rights;
- cemetery and chapel connections;
- inherited local Boundaries;
- pre-industrial maps.

Different houses may be modernizing, indebted, reactionary, commercially integrated or nearly ruined.

A house may retain powerful Name while losing much of its actual property Bond.

### 6.19.8 Worker mutual-aid and labor networks

Workers maintain bottom-up identity infrastructure through:

- mutual-aid societies;
- burial funds;
- strike organizations;
- neighborhood associations;
- literacy groups;
- household and contribution records.

These organizations can preserve Identity where official registration is weak or hostile.

They are especially important for Eastbank because worker families and mutual societies may preserve household Memory absent from State or corporate records.

### 6.19.9 Press and telegraph

The telegraph network primarily controls speed, synchronization and transmission.

Major newspapers interpret events and create rapid Public Belief.

Large newspapers therefore require abnormal fact-checking discipline because a widely repeated wrong Name or event framing can become a powerful Belief Anchor before Truth settles.

Journalists gain unusual gameplay relevance through the combination of access and mass publication.

### 6.19.10 Grey Registry network

The underground identity economy is a decentralized **Grey Registry**, not a single criminal guild.

Participants may include:

- document forgers;
- smugglers;
- corrupt or retired clerks;
- cemetery staff;
- former clergy;
- archive thieves;
- antique dealers;
- lawyers;
- dock fixers;
- confidence criminals.

The network trades in gaps between official systems:

- unregistered persons;
- still-active identities of the dead;
- obsolete passports;
- incomplete marriage release;
- broken title chains;
- missing files;
- ownerless relics;
- outdated railway passes;
- temporarily usable Names.

Its core product is access to reality not fully covered by official stabilization.

### 6.19.11 Institutional relationship model

Institutions must depend on and constrain one another.

Examples:

- abnormal investigators need Academy analysis and Church jurisdiction;
- Academy needs State funding, cases and records;
- Church relies on civil recognition while resisting secular encroachment;
- finance relies on court enforceability and accurate registry;
- rail relies on State charter, Capital and labor;
- old estates rely on Church and historic documentation but may owe modern lenders;
- labor networks rely on community recognition against corporate and administrative pressure;
- press relies on institutional sources while threatening all of them through exposure;
- Grey Registry survives on the gaps every formal institution creates.

No institution is designated as the canonical good or evil faction.

### 6.19.12 Eastbank evidence distribution

Eastbank evidence is intentionally distributed across institutions.

- **Abnormal-investigation authority** — decades of later anomaly cases but little original evidence.
- **University** — original or near-original anomaly studies, partly restricted.
- **Church** — burial, parish and sacred-status records containing names absent from official rolls.
- **Insurance / finance** — dense household, property and claim cross-records whose release could destabilize current ownership.
- **Rail corporation** — movement, timetable and worker records that may preserve obsolete spatial interpretations.
- **Old estates** — pre-industrial boundary and title records.
- **Worker organizations** — household Memory, mutual-aid ledgers and oral continuity.
- **Press** — reporting drafts and observations created before the Settlement narrative stabilized.
- **Grey Registry** — identities intentionally excluded from, hidden from or exploited around the Settlement.

No one archive solves the mystery.

### 6.19.13 Relationship state must not collapse into one reputation number

Future gameplay should distinguish at least:

- **Institutional Standing** — formal organizational view of the player;
- **Personal Relations** — individual NPC attitudes and trust;
- **Access** — actual permissions, credentials and doors the player can use;
- **Obligations** — duties, favors, debts, contracts or promises;
- **Known History** — what the institution believes it knows about the player's actions.

These may diverge.

A player can be formally banned while secretly trusted by one researcher, or institutionally respected while personally hated by a key investigator.

### 6.19.14 Institutions act without the player

Each major institution maintains its own Agenda and progresses over time.

Representative agendas:

- abnormal-investigation authority — prepare for new national abnormal-reporting rules;
- University — publish or suppress a new Identity theory;
- Church — contest civil marriage and registration reform;
- finance conglomerate — shape corporation and insurance law;
- railway corporation — expand or acquire old Eastbank land;
- labor organizations — pursue working-time, compensation or recognition reforms;
- press — investigate public corruption or institutional secrecy;
- Grey Registry — survive the coming national identity consolidation.

The player can alter these trajectories but does not cause them to exist.

### 6.19.15 Reality Consolidation Act as shared institutional pressure

The coming national Reality Consolidation program should intersect every major institution.

It promises:

- standardized identity;
- standardized death registration;
- civil marriage;
- corporate reform;
- abnormal-incident reporting;
- tighter administrative interoperability.

It threatens:

- Church jurisdiction;
- old estate rights;
- informal labor and neighborhood systems;
- migrant identities;
- Grey Registry survival;
- legacy corporate contracts;
- local custom.

The Eastbank Settlement is the city's historical precedent for why rapid consolidation may be both necessary and dangerous.

This connects the national reform storyline to the city's buried history.

### 6.19.16 Round 3.9 decision

Approved:

- ordinary police remain relevant;
- public specialist abnormal-investigation authority;
- modern city university and applied occult research;
- metropolitan Church plus specialist sacred-continuity office;
- major insurance / banking conglomerate with exceptional Eastbank records;
- rail / port / industry corporation with Institutional Identity risk;
- fragmented old-estate network;
- worker mutual-aid and labor Anchor networks;
- press and telegraph as distinct information functions;
- decentralized Grey Registry identity market;
- interdependence rather than isolated faction alignment;
- Eastbank evidence distributed across the entire institutional network;
- relationship state split into Standing / Personal Relations / Access / Obligations / Known History;
- independent institutional Agendas;
- Reality Consolidation Act as common pressure linking present politics to Eastbank.

Rejected:

- a single universal faction-reputation score;
- one “good institution” containing the real answer;
- a single villain faction secretly causing every city problem;
- supernatural cases making ordinary police and civil procedure irrelevant;
- treating worker and neighborhood organizations as background-only populations.
